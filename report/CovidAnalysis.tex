    \section{Materials and Methods}\label{sec:materials-and-methods}

    \subsection{Datasets} \label{subsec:datasets}

    Two separate gene count files from an RNAseq experiment of COVID-19 data were used for analysis. These files were generated using two different RNA-seq read alignment tools, STAR\cite{dobin_davis_schlesinger_drenkow_zaleski_jha_batut_chaisson_gingeras_2012} and HISAT2\cite{kim_paggi_park_bennett_salzberg_2019}. The gene count files produced by these aligners will be referred to as \textit{star} and \textit{hisat}, respectively, throughout the rest of this document.

    The samples in the dataset are categorized into three main experimental conditions:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{S2} - SARS-CoV-2 infected cells
        \item \textbf{S1} - SARS-CoV infected cells
        \item \textbf{mock} - Mock-infected cells
    \end{itemize}

    For both \textbf{S1} and \textbf{S2} infections, several time points post-infection were measured: 4 hours (4h), 12 hours (12h), and 24 hours (24h). For the \textbf{mock} condition, two time points were measured: 4 hours (4h) and 24 hours (24h).

    \subsection{Differential Gene Expression Analysis} \label{subsec:differential-gene-expression-analysis}
    Differential gene expression analysis was performed using the R package DESeq2\cite{love_huber_anders_2014}. The analysis was performed separately for the \textit{star} and \textit{hisat} datasets. The analysis was performed for each time point separately, comparing the gene expression levels between the infected and mock-infected cells. The analysis was performed accoding the steps described in Modern Statistics for Modern Biology, Chapter 8\cite{holmes_huber_2018}.

    The heatmap visualizes the top 30 variable genes, identified based on the highest variance in expression across samples. Variance-stabilized counts were calculated using the rlog transformation, which stabilizes variance across genes and accounts for differences in sequencing depth.
    The mean expression value for each gene was subtracted from its rlog-transformed counts to center the data. This normalization highlights deviations from the mean, with positive values indicating higher-than-average expression and negative values indicating lower-than-average expression.
    Hierarchical clustering was applied to group genes and samples based on expression similarities. The clustering revealed:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item At 4 hours post-infection, mock, S1, and S2 samples cluster together, reflecting minimal transcriptional divergence.
        \item At 12 and 24 hours, samples cluster by time point, with S1 and S2 grouping within each time cluster, indicating time-dependent transcriptional changes.
        \item Notable anomalies include a uniquely variable gene in mock and S1 at 24 hours (STAR dataset) and heightened variability in S2 at 24 hours, suggesting a distinct response.
    \end{itemize}
